Music in business videos is where a lot of small businesses get caught out. The song that is trending on your personal feed is often not available once you switch to a business account, and when it is available, the licence behind it usually was not written for advertising. Then you put the same clip on your website, where no platform licence covers you at all.

The short answer: for anything that promotes your business, use music from a library that is cleared for commercial use. That means Meta's Sound Collection on Facebook and Instagram, TikTok's Commercial Music Library, YouTube's Audio Library, or a paid production music licence that covers every place the video will go. A chart song needs permission from the people who own it, and for a small business that is rarely worth chasing.

This is not legal advice. It is how I handle music on the [video](/video.html) work I do, and what the platforms themselves say.

## Why the song you want is missing on a business account

Instagram's help page on its [licensed music library](https://help.instagram.com/402084904469945) is plain about it. The music in that library is "intended for personal, non-commercial use", and to keep it that way, certain business accounts and certain types of posts do not get access to it. That is why a café owner can find a song on their personal account and not on the café's.

TikTok goes further. Its help article on the [Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/commercial-music-library?lang=en) says businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial use. Business accounts are pointed to the commercial library instead, which covers organic posts, ads and branded content.

Switching the account back to personal is not a way around it. TikTok's wording is about commercial use, not the account setting, and a Reel selling your product is commercial whichever account posts it.

## Which music libraries are safe for business videos?

Each big platform has a library that is cleared for business use. The catch is that each one is tied to its own platform.

| Library | What the platform says | Where you can use it |
|---|---|---|
| [Meta Sound Collection](https://www.facebook.com/sound/collection/) | Over 14,000 songs and sounds, royalty free, usable for commercial purposes like ads | Videos you create and share on Facebook and Instagram |
| [TikTok Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/commercial-music-library?lang=en) | A pre-cleared library of 1 million songs for organic posts, ads and branded content | TikTok |
| [YouTube Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en) | Copyright-safe music and sound effects that won't be claimed through Content ID | YouTube, with attribution where the track asks for it |

The Sound Collection figure and the commercial-use wording come from [Instagram's help page](https://help.instagram.com/402084904469945). YouTube adds two cautions worth repeating: it only vouches for tracks downloaded from its own Audio Library, not "royalty-free" music from other channels, and it says it cannot give legal guidance on music used off the platform.

So a track that is fine in a Reel is not automatically fine in a YouTube ad, and a YouTube Audio Library track is not automatically fine on Instagram. Pick the music for where the video will actually run.

## What about a song by a real artist?

Two separate copyrights sit inside every commercial recording: the song itself, and the recording of it. APRA AMCOS explains on its [synchronisation page](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/music-licences/select-a-licence/synchronisation) that putting music to video usually needs permission from the copyright owners, and that these sync licences are not handled by APRA AMCOS. You go to the music publisher for the song, and to whoever owns the sound recording, often the record company, for the master.

APRA AMCOS does hold licences with Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok that [allow content containing its music to be uploaded](https://www.apraamcos.com.au/about-us/news-and-events/everything-you-need-to-know-about-licensing-music-online) to those platforms. The same page says pre-recorded videos may still need other licences, including a sync licence. That is the gap a business ad falls into.

In practice, a sync licence for a well-known song costs more time and money than a short promo is worth. Unless the track is the whole idea of the campaign, use something cleared.

## What happens if you use it anyway

Usually nothing dramatic, at first. On YouTube, [Content ID](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en) scans every upload against files from copyright owners. A match can block the video, put ads on it with the money going to the owner, or just track it, and the result can differ from country to country.

None of that is a lawsuit. But a blocked or claimed video is one you paid for and cannot use, and an ad pulled the week of your sale costs more than the music would have.

The bigger risk is the version on your own site. Platform music deals stay on the platform. A Reel with a library track, downloaded and dropped onto your home page or into a YouTube ad, is outside what that library allowed.

## How I handle music on a shoot

I plan the music at the same time as the edit, because it decides the cut. For most small business work, that means one of three things:

1. **Licensed production music** that covers social, web and ads, chosen before the edit so the cuts land on the beat.
2. **Clean audio with no music**, so you can add a sound from the platform's commercial library inside the app. This is the route when you want to ride a sound that is trending on TikTok or Instagram, as long as it is in that platform's commercial library.
3. **Natural sound only.** A fight night corner, a car starting, a crowd. For event coverage like the frame at the top of this post, the room is often the best soundtrack you can get.

When a video goes to several places, I check that the music licence covers each of them before export. That matters most when one shoot is cut into the different [social media video sizes](/blog/social-media-video-sizes.html) for Reels, Shorts and a website banner. If a video is going on your site to [get found on Google](/blog/video-seo-small-business.html), it needs music you can use off-platform.

## A quick checklist before you post

- Is this video selling or promoting something? Then treat it as commercial.
- Where will it run? Pick a library cleared for that platform, or a licence that covers every one.
- Is it going on your website or into a paid ad as well? Platform libraries usually do not follow it there.
- Does the track need attribution? Put it in the description.
- Keep a note of where each track came from, with the licence, in case anyone asks.
