CME DetailingWebsite rebuild and local SEO

A detailer working across the Southern Highlands and south-west Sydney needed a site that matched the quality of the work and kept the search traffic it already had.

The brief

CME Detailing does ceramic coatings, paint protection film and detailing. The existing site had grown a large set of town pages over the years and was moving to new hosting. The brief had three parts: nothing broken, a site that looks good on a phone, and a design with its own character.

What was built

  • A generator, not a pile of pages. All copy lives in one data file and a build script writes every page, so a change to a price or a phone number is made once.
  • Service pages with substance. Each service explains the process, what is included and how long it takes, with a page for paint correction that the old site lacked.
  • Town pages worth keeping. Pages for the towns the business serves were rewritten, and pages for services with no realistic local search demand were retired and redirected.
  • Real reviews. Genuine Google reviews replaced generic testimonials, with star ratings drawn as vector graphics instead of text symbols.
  • No uploads, no clutter. A simple text enquiry form that reaches the owner directly.

Keeping the rankings

The careful part was the addresses. The first redirect map was checked against the site's own sitemap. It turned out Google had also indexed an older set of addresses from a previous version of the site, and those would have returned errors. The final build redirects every address the site has ever used, more than 300 rules in all, to its closest replacement.

The method is written up in the guide to moving a website without losing rankings.

Details that mattered

  • Links and assets are written relative to each page, so the site previews correctly from any folder or sub-path.
  • Stylesheets and scripts carry a version stamp, so a visitor is never served an out-of-date copy.
  • The map embed uses the street address alone, which pins more reliably than a business name.
  • A sweep at narrow widths caught an overlay that collided with other text on small phones.
